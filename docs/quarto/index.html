Predictors, Causes, and Outcomes of 30-Day Readmissions Following Mechanical Thrombectomy in Patients With Chronic Kidney Disease

RCOP C1 - Nationwide Readmissions Database, 2016–2022

Author
Affiliation

Dua Ali

Dow University of Health Sciences

Preamble

  • Reference Studies:

    • Morita et al., 2019

  • Objective: Estimate the national incidence of, and independent predictors of, 30-day unplanned readmission among adults with chronic kidney disease (CKD, stages 1-5) who undergo mechanical thrombectomy (EVT) for acute ischemic stroke, and characterize the most common causes and outcomes of those readmissions.

  • Data source: HCUP Nationwide Readmissions Database (NRD), 2016–2022 — a nationally representative, all-payer database that links a patient’s hospitalizations within a calendar year.

  • Cohort: Every qualifying index event is a non-elective admission among adults aged ≥ 18 with acute ischemic stroke in the principal or secondary diagnosis position, a mechanical thrombectomy procedure performed on the same stay, and chronic kidney disease (stages 1-5) present on the index admission. Dialysis-dependent end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) is modeled as a predictor.

  • Primary outcome: 30-day all-cause unplanned (non-elective) readmission, causes and costs for index admissions and 30-day readmissions.

  • Cost: Hospital costs were estimated by applying HCUP hospital-specific cost-to-charge ratios to reported charges, then adjusting to 2022 dollars using the Consumer Price Index (medical care component). All reported dollar figures represent estimated hospital costs, not billed charges, and refer to the readmission encounter itself.

Methods

All analyses accounted for the NRD complex survey design. Because NRD strata and hospital identifiers reset each year, the design used year-qualified strata (interaction of YEAR and NRD_STRATUM) and year-qualified primary sampling units (interaction of YEAR and HOSP_NRD), with DISCWT weights and a nested design; survey.lonely.psu = "adjust" was set before the design was built.

Baseline characteristics were summarized by 30-day readmission status as median (IQR) for continuous variables and n (weighted %) for categorical variables, with design-based Rao-Scott chi-square (categorical) or survey t-test (continuous) comparisons. Predictors of 30-day readmission were estimated with a survey-weighted logistic (quasibinomial) model including demographic and hospital covariates, discharge year as a fixed effect, index-admission clinical covariates (IV thrombolysis, intracranial hemorrhage/hemorrhagic transformation), and a pre-specified comorbidity panel, with no stepwise selection; adjusted odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals are reported. Readmission diagnoses were ranked by weighted frequency using raw (leaf-level) ICD-10-CM principal diagnosis codes; diagnoses appearing in fewer than 11 unweighted discharges are suppressed per HCUP small-cell rules. Index-admission outcomes (non-home discharge, length of stay, inflation-adjusted cost) were compared between patients with and without a 30-day readmission using design-based Rao-Scott chi-square (categorical) or Wilcoxon rank-sum (continuous, via svyranktest); in-hospital mortality at index admission is not reported, as survival to discharge is a cohort inclusion criterion. Analyses used R with the survey, srvyr, and gtsummary packages.
